FRZFRM Weather Catch

Catch the real weather where you are

  • Every kind of weather is a jar to collect. Rare weather is worth more points.
  • Areas are about 5 km across. You can catch each kind of weather once per area, every day.

    How catching works

    • The map is split into areas about 5 km across. The app checks the live weather for yours.
    • You can catch each kind of weather once per area, every day. Move to a new area, or wait for the weather to change, to find more.
    • Catching is a quick game: scoop rain or snow, hold to fill a jar with fog, time a tap for sun and wind, or tap the instant lightning flashes. From Uncommon up, weather can slip away if you miss, except named storms.
    • Points: Common 10, Uncommon 25, Rare 75, Epic 200, Legendary 500. The first catch of each kind of weather each day earns full points. Catch the same weather again that day, in another area, and it earns half. Points level you up, and levels unlock nearby weather, the forecast and early warnings.
    • Catch the weather, stay put, and be there when it turns, and you earn a Change moment like Sunbreak.
    • Sky Wonders like the full moon come from the sky calendar. Rainbows, halos and the Northern Lights are caught with a photo.
    • Named storms: when the Met Office, Met Éireann or KNMI names a storm, you can catch it while it’s over the UK and Ireland, in gusts of 35 mph or more, or rain with gusts of 25 mph. The jar takes the storm’s name, like Storm Boelo, and each name only comes once.
    • The weather data can miss a passing shower or a patch of fog. If you can see weather the app hasn’t picked up, tap Spot something. The app checks the weather around you first.
    • Catch the same weather again to level its jar up: bronze at 2 catches, silver at 5, gold at 15 and rainbow at 30. Each level gives the jar a new lid. Every catch counts and nothing is used up: every variant you find stays in the jar.
    • Mystery variants: every jar opens as Classic (1 in 2 jars) or one of six surprises picked to fit that jar’s weather, from 1 in 6 to 1 in 40, like a kite on a breezy day or a snowman in the snow. Rarest of all is each jar’s legendary (1 in 60), like the Green Flash at sunset, Sunbeams through the clouds or a Yeti in a blizzard. Tap any jar, even one you haven’t caught yet, to see all eight. Each catch is drawn its own way too, so even when everyone nearby catches the same storm, your jar won’t look quite like theirs.
    • Jar looks: in Shelf style, pick a jar look for each section of your shelf, like Sea Glass for Rainy or Frosted Glass for Snowy & Icy. New looks unlock as you level up.
    • Photo catches are checked on your phone: the photo has to show the right kind of sky, like a dark night with a green glow for the Northern Lights, and it can’t have a face in it. Nothing is sent anywhere. The check can miss a face, so if a photo of a person ever gets into a jar, delete it in Your photos below.
    • Flying? Keep the app open on the plane. When the sun sets while you’re flying, you can catch Sunset Over the Clouds. Your phone’s GPS can tell you’re flying, even without wifi.
    • Levels: tap your level to see what’s ahead: new titles, new powers and new looks for your shelf. The next ones are shown in full, and the rest are only hinted at.
    • Animals: scan a real animal with your camera and it goes in its jar. There are garden animals, pets, and beach, countryside and zoo animals. The photo is looked at on your phone and thrown away. Each animal can be caught once every 30 minutes, and it earns points by how rare it is: 5 for a seagull, up to 100 for a zoo animal, and a fifth of that for the same animal again that day. Its first catch moves into its everyday home. After that, each catch turns up in one of its five homes (1 in 2 up to 1 in 24 for its favourite). Each pet counts once: scanning your own dog again won’t level up the Dog jar, so find different dogs, cats and so on. To tell pets apart, the app keeps a short list of numbers about each pet you catch, on your phone only, never the photo. Two pets that look almost the same might be taken for one.
    • Hide & seek: in the Animals tab, find the animal hiding in a busy picture, among things its own colours, like a brown dog among shoes. There are hundreds of pictures, and when one comes round again the animal is somewhere new. The pictures get harder the quicker you find the animals, and a little easier if you need a hint. The first 10 finds each day earn points: 6 for a find within 20 seconds, 4 within a minute, then 2. A hint halves them.
    • Strength: each catch gets 1 to 5 stars for how strong the weather really was, like how hard it rained, how hot it got, how deep the snow was or how thick the fog. 4 stars are worth half as many points again, and 5 stars double. Each jar remembers its strongest catch.
    • Shelf rewards: every 10 jars unlocks a new wood or lights for your shelf, with bonus points. Finish a row and that shelf changes for good. Get every jar in a row to gold and the shelf turns gold. Fill the whole shelf to earn the Sky Jar.

    Drive mode

    Switch it on before you set off and put your phone in a holder with the app open. Once you’ve driven out of the area you set off from, it seals the weather on your journey into jars, with nothing to touch: each kind of weather once a trip, up to 5 jars, keeping the rarest. Open them when you’ve stopped. Trip jars earn half points, as there’s no catching game. Passengers can play as normal.

    Keep the app open on screen, ideally plugged in. A website only gets your location while it’s open, so if the screen turns off or you open another app, the trip stops following you.

    About

    FRZFRM Weather Catch, test version . Some rare weather is worked out from forecasts. Photos are checked on the phone, for faces and for the right kind of sky, and animals are recognised on the phone too.

    Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0). Place names from BigDataCloud. Faces are found with OpenCV’s LBP face cascade (BSD licence). Animals are recognised with OpenCLIP (ViT-B/32 trained on LAION-2B, MIT licence), running on your phone.
